## Abstract

There has been relentless debate as to whether dyslexia is a gift, and specifically, if dyslexics are more creative than the average person, despite established research evidence that there is no difference in creativity between those with and without dyslexia. With this paper, I outline that this conversation is not that simple, and one must consider society’s view of disability and the developmental experience of growing up in a society that devalues dyslexic people. I conclude by positing that while dyslexia is not a gift, it is a part of neurodiversity.

## Highlights

1) This paper describes the nuances as to why, while dyslexia is not a gift, it is not that simple.

2) As dyslexics develop, they exist within an ableist society that devalues people who are struggling readers, disabled, and neurodivergent.

3) Dyslexia does not need to come with special gifts in order for dyslexics to hold value in society. Dyslexics are part of neurodiversity, and neurodiversity is valued.

## Dyslexia is not a Gift, but it is not that Simple

Discourse on whether dyslexia is a gift has become common in conversations surrounding dyslexia, particularly on the topic of creativity. Some dyslexics are referring to dyslexia as a gift as a way to assert that dyslexics are valuable members of society (Gobbo, 2010; Paul, 2012; Cancer & Antonietti, 2020), which is paralleled by quantitative research establishing dyslexia does not come with gifted skills (Erbeli et al., 2022; Gutiérrez-Ortega et al., 2023). With this, conceptualizations of dyslexia are being limited to one of two incorrect boxes: dyslexics are either gifted or incapable (Kirby, 2022). Because of this, recent discourse about whether or not dyslexia is a “gift” has been unproductive at best and feeding resentment at worst, and ultimately has not been leading to a more complete understanding of dyslexia.

In this paper, I, a dyslexic<sup>1</sup> dyslexia researcher, outline that while dyslexia is not a gift, it is not that simple. We need to reframe our conceptualizations about dyslexia, away from a gift or medical perspective and towards a neurodiversity perspective. I contend that dyslexics hold value, not because they possess special gifts, but in that no group should be devalued on the basis of (dis)ability or neurotype. Instead of claiming dyslexia is a “gift” that makes people more “creative,” a more accurate wording is that dyslexics are “neurodivergent” and are a part of the “neurodiversity” that adds value to our world. Neurodiversity refers to the biological fact that there is variation in the human mind; every person is a part of neurodiversity (Ne’eman & Pellicano, 2022). Within neurodiversity, neurodivergent people are those with brains that deviate in function from what would be considered typical (Walker, 2014; Asasumasu, 2015).

关于阅读障碍是否是一种天赋的讨论，在围绕阅读障碍的对话中已变得司空见惯，尤其是在创造力这一话题上。一些阅读障碍者将阅读障碍称为一种天赋，以此主张阅读障碍者是社会中有价值的成员（Gobbo，2010；Paul，2012；Cancer 与 Antonietti，2020）；与此同时，定量研究却表明阅读障碍并不伴随特殊的天赋技能（Erbeli 等，2022；Gutiérrez‑Ortega 等，2023）。由此，对阅读障碍的概念化被局限在两个错误的框定之中：阅读障碍者要么天赋异禀，要么能力不足（Kirby，2022）。正因如此，近期关于阅读障碍是否为“天赋”的讨论，往好里说是徒劳无功，往坏里说则助长了怨恨情绪，最终未能促成对阅读障碍更全面的理解。

在本文中，我作为一名阅读障碍者兼阅读障碍研究者，阐明尽管阅读障碍并非一种天赋，但问题远非如此简单。我们需要重新构建对阅读障碍的概念化理解，从“天赋”或医学视角转向神经多样性视角。我认为，阅读障碍者的价值并不在于他们拥有特殊的天赋，而在于任何群体都不应基于（残）障能力或神经类型而被贬低。与其声称阅读障碍是一种让人更“富有创造力”的“天赋”，更准确的表述是：阅读障碍者属于“神经殊异”群体，是为我们世界增添价值的“神经多样性”的一部分。神经多样性指的是人类心智存在差异这一生物学事实；每个人都是神经多样性的一部分（Ne'eman &Pellicano, 2022）。在神经多样性范畴内，神经殊异人士是指其大脑功能偏离所谓典型标准的人群（Walker, 2014; Asasumasu, 2015）。

## 阅读障碍的概念化

To understand why this debate of dyslexic gifts is occurring, we must first take a step back to consider various conceptualizations of what it means to be dyslexic. There are several models of disability that inform the way that people conceptualize what disability is; see Smart and Smart (2006) for an overview. Much of quantitative dyslexia research rests on the medical model of disability. The medical model of disability reduces a disability down to solely the specific medical symptoms of that disability (Retief & Letšosa, 2018). There are other models of disability that, unlike the medical model, consider context, intersectional identities, personal experience, and identity formation. Below I discuss that while dyslexia is a medical condition, being dyslexic is, more importantly, also an identity.

## Dyslexia as a medical condition

Dyslexia, according to the Diagnostic and Statistical Manual for Mental Disorders, is a “specific learning disorder with impairments in reading” (American Psychiatric Association, 2013, p. 67). Dyslexia is neurologically based and phenotypically characterized by difficulties related to reading such as low decoding, low fluency, and difficulty in spelling (Lyon et al., 2003). Dyslexia is categorized as a disability as it substantially limits individuals in at least one major area of life (Americans with Disabilities Act, 1990). Although this medical definition is helpful for diagnosis and placement into important interventions (American Psychiatric Association, 2013), this conceptualization is medicalized, homogenous, and lacks dimension.

A more complete understanding of dyslexia would include the experience of living in the world as a dyslexic. ## 作为医学状况的阅读障碍

根据精神障碍诊断与统计手册，阅读障碍是一种“阅读障碍型特定学习障碍”（美国精神医学学会，2013 年，第 67 页）。阅读障碍具有神经学基础，其表型特征表现为与阅读相关的困难，如解码能力低、流畅性差以及拼写困难（Lyon 等，2003 年）。阅读障碍被归类为残障，因为它在至少一个主要生活领域对个体造成实质性限制（美国残疾人法案，1990 年）。尽管这一定义有助于诊断并将其纳入重要的干预措施（美国精神医学学会，2013 年），但这种概念化方式过于医学化、同质化且缺乏维度。

Children grow up within societies that are made up of contexts that inform all parts of their development (Bronfenbrenner, 1986). Dyslexic children are developing within the context of an ableist society that devalues dyslexia, neurodiversity, and disability (Daley & Rappolt-Schlichtmann, 2018; Haft et al., 2022). Dyslexics go through school often feeling isolated, misunderstood, and questioning their worth (Hernandez-Saca, 2016). Through these experiences dyslexics form their understanding of dyslexia and the wider world, bringing these insights with them into every interaction.

## The dyslexia gift debate

Ableism is the belief system of valuing certain abilities over others (Wolbring, 2008). Ableism has long been used to justify prejudice against marginalized groups perceived to have lower abilities, including disabled people (Wolbring, 2008). When confronted with ableist assumptions of the value disabled people hold, a common reaction is to realign with ableness (and neurotypicality) as opposed to questioning the contexts of the society that questioned disabled people’s value in the first place (Brune & Wilson, 2013). Some well-intentioned dyslexics, parents, educators, and advocates of dyslexics reacted by claiming dyslexia is a “gift,” specifically that dyslexics are more creative, finding comfort and validation in this unfounded idea. Today, this idea that dyslexia is a gift has started to enter mainstream understandings of dyslexia, even appearing in popular media (Paul, 2012; Kirby, 2022). Much of this gift narrative is powered by self-report and limited research evidence (Chakravarty, 2009; Eide & Eide, 2011; Chamberlain et al., 2018). ## 阅读障碍天赋之争

健全中心主义是一种认为某些能力优于其他能力的信念体系（Wolbring，2008）。长期以来，健全中心主义被用来为针对被视为能力较低的边缘化群体（包括残障人士）的偏见辩护（Wolbring，2008）。当面对关于残障人士价值的健全中心主义假设时，一种常见的反应是重新认同健全性（以及神经典型性），而非质疑最初对残障人士价值提出质疑的社会背景（Brune 与 Wilson，2013）。一些善意的阅读障碍者、家长、教育工作者以及阅读障碍者的倡导者对此的反应是声称阅读障碍是一种“天赋”，具体而言即阅读障碍者更具创造力，并在这种毫无根据的观点中寻求安慰和认可。如今，这种将阅读障碍视为天赋的观点已开始进入主流对阅读障碍的理解，甚至出现在大众媒体中（Paul，2012；Kirby，2022）。这一“天赋”叙事很大程度上依赖于自我报告和有限的研究证据（Chakravarty，2009；Eide 与 Eide，2011；Chamberlain 等，2018）。然而，目前已有确凿的研究驳斥了这些主张，

## Broken communication

It is important to consider how this assertion that dyslexia is not a gift is communicated to the dyslexic community. When non-dyslexic researchers publish these findings without taking the time to honor dyslexic identity, pride, and neurodiversity, it may feel like an attack on the value of dyslexics and neurodiversity, reaffirming the low self-worth dyslexics struggle with. This becomes an issue of communication, and thus, the currently unproductive discourse of gifts continues to transpire.

Those on each side of this discourse, over if dyslexia is a gift, are talking past each other and being driven apart in the process. On one side, there are those pointing to the lived experiences of dyslexics stating dyslexia is a gift in an attempt to prove dyslexic people have value. In response I ask, why does dyslexia need to come with special gifts for dyslexics to hold value in society? On the other side, others are pointing to quantitative data stating dyslexia is not a gift. To those I ask, has time been taken to consider the experience of dyslexics who often feel misunderstood and spoken over by the way that research is constructed, reported, and communicated to a wider non-researcher audience?

## A solution: Research partnerships

“Nothing About Us, Without Us,” recently updated to “Nothing Without Us,” is a tenet of the Neurodiversity and Disability Justice movement (Global Disability Summit, 2022). This phrase is a demand by disabled people that nothing pertaining to disability should be done without the active involvement of disabled people. Historically, researchers of disability have not included disabled people in conversations regarding disability beyond being study subjects

## 沟通断裂

重要的是，需考量“阅读障碍并非天赋”这一论断是如何传达给阅读障碍群体的。当非阅读障碍研究者发布此类研究结果，却未花时间尊重阅读障碍身份认同、自豪感及神经多样性时，这可能被视为对阅读障碍者价值及神经多样性的攻击，进而强化了阅读障碍者所挣扎的低自我价值感。这成为一个沟通问题，因此，目前关于天赋的无益讨论仍在持续上演。

在这场关于阅读障碍是否为天赋的讨论中，双方各执一词，彼此误解，渐行渐远。一方援引阅读障碍者的生活经历，声称阅读障碍是一种天赋，试图以此证明阅读障碍者具有价值。对此我要问：为何阅读障碍者必须拥有特殊天赋，才能在社会中体现其价值？另一方则引用定量数据，断言阅读障碍并非天赋。对此我也要问：是否曾花时间考量过阅读障碍者的感受？他们常因研究的设计、报告及向更广泛非研究受众传播的方式而感到被误解、被忽视。

## 解决方案：研究伙伴关系

“没有我们参与，不要谈论我们”最近更新为“没有我们，一事无成”，这是神经多样性与残障正义运动的一项原则（全球残障峰会，2022）。这句话是残障人士提出的要求：任何涉及残障的事务，若无残障人士的积极参与，便不应推进。历史上，残障研究者在与残障相关的讨论中，往往未将残障人士纳入其中，仅将其视为研究对象。

(Sarju, 2021) and today the majority of dyslexia researchers are not dyslexic themselves. When people come together from a range of diverse backgrounds and neurotypes, they each can bring different perspectives and ways of approaching the same topic; this is the benefit of neurodiversity (Walker, 2014; Elsherif et al., 2022). The topic of dyslexic gifts is just one example of the need for neurodiverse perspectives, and specifically dyslexic perspectives. Dyslexic people bring important perspectives of what it means to be dyslexic. It is fine for nondyslexic people to research dyslexia, but dyslexia research should be done in collaboration with the dyslexic community. Research is best conducted as a collaborative team (Hall et al., 2018; Patton Terry et al., 2021), and enhanced when those research teams are neurodiverse in membership (Gourdon-Kanhukamwe et al., 2023; Manalili et al., 2023). The field of dyslexia research needs to move towards actively forming research partnerships with dyslexic people. By conducting dyslexia research in the format of collaborative teams that are neurodiverse in membership, dyslexia research can be designed, carried out, and communicated in ways that result in more nuanced understandings of the dyslexic experience.

## Conclusion

There are currently no known specific gifts intrinsic to dyslexia at the population level (Erbeli et al., 2022), but it is not that simple. As dyslexics develop, they experience the world as dyslexics, as struggling readers, and as neurodivergent disabled children often living in the context of an ableist world that misunderstands and devalues them for their disability. Dyslexia does not need to be a gift for dyslexic people to be valuable. Dyslexic people have inherent value and add to neurodiversity.

## 结论

目前尚无已知特定于阅读障碍人群层面的内在天赋（Erbeli 等，2022），但情况并非如此简单。随着阅读障碍者的成长，他们以阅读障碍者、阅读困难者以及神经殊异残障儿童的身份体验世界，而这类儿童往往生活在健全主义世界中，该世界因他们的残障而误解并贬低其价值。阅读障碍无需成为一种天赋，阅读障碍者才具有价值。阅读障碍者拥有内在价值，并为神经多样性做出贡献。
