摘要：孤独谱系常被视为一种特定类型的心智。主流神经认知主义方法通过以过度系统化和低共情为框架的静态处理特征来解释这一点。相比之下，受维特根斯坦启发的评论家认为，孤独谱系的连贯性来自关系性的主体间性破坏，这种破坏阻碍了进入语言意义的共享世界。本文认为这两派都过度简化目与新出现的证据相冲突，部分原因在于不公正地假设了基于缺陷的孤独谱系框架。随后，本文发展了一种新的维特根斯坦式论述——孤独谱系作为一种不同的生命形式——从而避免了这些问题。孤独谱系系统化并非孤独谱系认知的基础而是被视为对前认知和语义焦虑的反应，这些焦虑源于在一种不同生命形式中作为少数群体成长的过程。这种重新框架化能够提供对孤独谱系心智的连贯描述，并具有重要的概念、实践与伦理含义。

关键词：孤独谱系、孤独谱系、维特根斯坦、过度系统化、生活形式、心智、心理学理论、确定性、认知。

## 初步论述

孤独谱系障碍的诊断参照两个关键行为标准：（1）社交与沟通问题，（2）受限、重复且特异的习惯、行为与常规。然而，孤独谱系——我在此将如此称呼它——并不仅仅是一组行为。事实上，它极其复杂，并与“一个由能力与局限构成的多维流形”密切相关（Hacking 2010, 265）。此外，正如心理学家乌塔·弗里斯所指出的，心智是孤独谱系的“核心”，而只有当我们“了解孤独谱系是什么样子”时，孤独谱系行为才能被理解（Frith 2008,65）。与弗里斯一致，我在此关注的是我们可称之为孤独谱系心智的东西——即，孤独谱系被理解为一种不同的思维方式、理解方式和与世界的关联方式，而不仅仅是一组相对于精神病学规范被视为功能失调的行为集合。<sup>1</sup>

# AUTISM AS A FORM OF LIFE: WITTGENSTEIN AND THE PSYCHOLOGICAL COHERENCE OF AUTISM

ROBERT CHAPMAN

Abstract: Autism is often taken to be a specific kind of mind. The dominant neuro-cognitivist approach explains this via static processing traits framed in terms of hyper-systemising and hypo-empathising. By contrast, Wittgenstein inspired commentators argue that the coherence of autism arises relationally, from intersubjective disruption that hinders access to a shared world of linguistic meaning. This paper argues that both camps are unduly reductionistic and conflict with emerging evidence, due in part to unjustifiably assuming a deficit based framing of autism. It then develops a new Wittgensteinian account—autism as a different form of life—which avoids these issues. Rather than autistic systemising being the basis of autistic cognition, it is taken to be a reaction to preepistemic and semantic anxieties that come with developing as a minority within a different form of life. This re-framing can provide a coherent account of the autistic mind, and has significant conceptual, practical, and ethical implications.

Keywords: autism, autistic, Wittgenstein, hyper-systemising, form of life, mind, psychological theory, certainty, cognition.

## First Remarks

Autism Spectrum Disorder is diagnosed with reference to two key be havioural criteria: (1) social and communication problems and (2) restricted, repetitive, and idiosyncratic habits, actions, and routines. But autism—as I shall call it—is not simply a set of behaviours. In fact, it is highly complex and related to a “many-dimensional manifold of abilities and limitations” (Hacking 2010, 265). Moreover, as psychologist Uta Frith points out, the mind is the “heart” of autism, and autistic behaviour will only be understood if we “know what it is like to be autistic” (Frith 2008, 65). In line with Frith my concern here is what we might call the autistic mind—that is, autism when conceived as a different way of thinking, understanding, and relating to the world, not merely as a cluster of be haviours deemed dysfunctional with respect to psychiatric norms.

对孤独谱系心智的主流框架来自我称之为神经认知论者的群体。根据这一观点，孤独谱系差异可以直接追溯到并很大程度上由所有孤独谱系个体固有的静态神经认知差异解释（例如，Baron‑Cohen 2008；Vermeulen 2001）。尽管这种框架占主导地位，神经和认知研究却产生了许多相互矛盾的发现，我稍后会谈到，这使得这一框架难以维持。我将把认知论框架的主要替代方案称为维特根斯坦式框架。维特根斯坦式解释不同于认知主义解释，因为它们——遵循维特根斯坦——认为人类思维和语言只能出现在共享的“生活形式”（1953，§241）及其各种生活形式的共享关注和实践中，而不是植根于个体信息处理。根据其支持者的说法，这一替代框架的优势之一在于，它使我们能够将孤独谱系视为具有统一性质，同时与科学发现保持一致。

然而，正如我下文详细讨论的那样，这种维特根斯坦式的替代方案本身也存在问题。本文旨在表明，尽管存在这些不足，借鉴维特根斯坦的思想来理解孤独谱系思维，仍然可以大有裨益，从而克服以往解释所面临的问题。我的分析借鉴了维特根斯坦关于方面盲、确定性和对普遍性的渴望——这些术语我将在适当时候加以解释——的工作，旨在阐明孤独谱系思维源于一种不同而非缺陷的生活形式。我将表明，这种解释具有强大的解释力，并能提供洞见，以改善孤独谱系个体的生存机遇。事实上，我们将看到，维特根斯坦在这两个方面所提出的见解，远多于以往所承认的。

## 神经认知论解释

首先，有必要花些时间讨论目前仍主导孤独谱系研究的神经认知主义框架。在这一框架下，人类思维类似于计算机处理，而孤独谱系心智则涉及一种不同于神经典型情况的操作系统（认知）和硬件（神经）。大多数认知主义解释的一个核心主张是，与被认为具有完整能力的神经典型群体相比，孤独谱系者天生在“共情”或“心智化”能力上较为欠缺（Baron‑Cohen 1995；Frith 2003 [1989]）。这意味着，与神经典型群体相比，孤独谱系个体更难以处理社会世界，并且最核心的是，他们在认知他心存在方面存在困难。

反过来，一般的孤独谱系认知被描述为“超系统化”或“超机械化”（Baron‑Cohen 2011；Badcock 2009）。这大致意味着，孤独谱系个体擅长处理

The dominant way of framing the autistic mind comes from those I shall call the neuro-cognitivists. On this view, autistic differences are directly traceable back to, and largely explained by, static neuro-cognitive differences that are inherent to all autistic individuals (see, e.g., Baron-Cohen 2008; Vermeulen 2001). Despite its dominance, neurological and cognitive research has produced many conflicting findings that, as I shal come back to, make this framing hard to sustain. I shall refer to the lead ing alternative to the cognitivist framing as the Wittgensteinian framing. Wittgensteinian accounts differ from the cognitivist accounts because— following Wittgenstein—they take human thought and language to be something that can only occur within a shared “form of life” (1953, § 241) and the various shared concerns and practices of its lifeforms, rather than having its seat in individual information processing. According to its pro ponents, one of the strengths of this alternative framing is that it allows us to see autism as having a unified nature whilst simultaneously remaining consistent with scientific findings.

As I discuss at length below, however, this Wittgensteinian alter native has problems of its own. My aim in this paper is to show that, despite these shortcomings, there is still much to be gained by drawing on Wittgenstein for understanding autistic thinking in such a way that overcomes the problems associated with previous accounts. My own anal ysis draws on Wittgenstein’s work on aspect-blindness, certainty, and the craving for generality—terms I explain in due course—in order to clarify autistic thinking as arising from a different, but not deficient, form of life. I show that this has strong explanatory power and can provide insight into how to improve the chances of thriving for autistic individuals. Indeed, as we shall see, Wittgenstein has a lot more to say in both of these regards than has previously been acknowledged.

## The Neuro-Cognitivist Account

To begin with, it will be worth spending some time discussing the neu ro-cognitivist framework, which still dominates autism studies. On this framework, human thinking is analogous to computer processing, with the autistic mind involving something like an operating system (cogni tion) and hardware (neurology) different to the neurotypical case. A core claim for most cognitivist accounts is that autistics have an inherently diminished capacity for “empathising” or “mentalising” when compared to neurotypicals, who are taken to have an intact capacity (Baron-Cohen 1995; Frith 2003 [1989]). What this means is that, when compared to neurotypicals, autistic individuals find it harder to process the social world and, most centrally, struggle to cognise the existence of other minds.

In turn, general autistic cognition has been characterised as “hypersystemizing” or “hyper-mechanistic” (Baron-Cohen 2011; Badcock 2009)This means, roughly, that autistic individuals are good at processing固定的、系统化的信息片段，但在处理其他任何信息时则要差得多。与此同时，孤独谱系感知处理被描述为“语境盲”或缺乏“中央一致性”（Vermeulen 2012；Frith 2003 [1989]）。这意味着，孤独谱系大脑在处理意义时对相关语境的敏感度低于神经典型大脑。这导致对细节的关注以牺牲整体为代价，使得孤独谱系个体往往错过某些意义的要点，并导致一个碎片化感知世界。

我认为这一框架之所以占据主导地位有两个核心原因。首先，它从内部视角清晰解释了孤独谱系体验和孤独谱系行为。例如，许多孤独谱系群体报告自己以高度系统化的方式思考，能注意到他人忽略的细节（Badcock 2009），存在感觉敏感性、感觉整合差异，以及体验到一个“未经过滤”的世界（Bogdashina 2016）。因此，那些试图以共通的潜在认知和感知特质来清晰解释这些体验的论述，旨在解释孤独谱系群体似乎所体验到的世界。反过来，神经认知主义框架可以为孤独谱系行为提供解释。社交和沟通问题被认为源于根本性的认知差异，因为共情需要语境敏感性（Vermeulen 2012），而且社交世界并不受清晰、固定的规则支配（Baron‑Cohen 2011）。最后，孤独谱系个体的高度重复行为被解释为具有高度系统化的处理风格（Baron‑Cohen 2011）。显然，这种看似强大的解释力正是认知主义论述吸引人的一部分原因。

认知主义解释的第二个优势在于其实用性。总体而言，孤独谱系分类从医学角度来看一直缺乏实用性（Hassal 2017）。但认知主义解释取得了一定的成功，这得益于基于特定孤独谱系认知理论开发出的赋能且非侵入性的教育技巧。其中一个例子基于孤独谱系的超系统化理论，是一张互动DVD，帮助孤独谱系群体通过面部表情学习理解情绪（Baron‑Cohen 2003；Golan 和 Baron‑Cohen 2006）。该DVD中，六名演员各自展示了412个明确标注的表情，让观看者能够记住这些表情，然后系统地将其映射到现实世界中，以便更好地在其中运作。另一个例子基于孤独谱系的语境盲理论，旨在向孤独谱系儿童教授苏格拉底方法，帮助他们学会处理概念性问题、掌握模糊概念，并改善其整体运作能力和生活方式（Vermeulen 2013）。这种方法之所以有效，是因为它运用反思性逻辑思维来理解那些需要强语境敏感性才能自发理解的复杂范畴和概念（Vermeulen 2012）。在这两个案例中，这两种原本截然不同的改善孤独谱系生活的方法都基于一个前提：典型的孤独谱系问题可以通过

At the same time, autistic perceptual processing has been portrayed as being “context blind” or as lacking “central coherence” (Vermeulen 2012; Frith 2003 [1989]). This means that autistic brains process meaning with less sensitivity to the relevant context than neurologically typical brains. This leads to a focus on details at the expense of wholes, such that autistic individuals tend to miss the gist of certain meanings, and to a fragmented sensory-perceptual world.

I take it there are two core reasons why this framework has dominated. First, it offers a clear explanation of autistic experience and behaviours from the inside out. Many autistic people report, for instance, thinking in a highly systematic way, noticing details that others miss (Badcock 2009), sensory sensitivities, sensory integration differences, and the experience of an “unfiltered” world (Bogdashina 2016). So, accounts that seek to clearly explain these experiences in terms of shared underlying cognitive and perceptual traits purport to explain the world as autistic people seem to experience it. In turn, the neuro-cognitivist framing can provide an explanation for autistic behaviours. Social and communication issues are taken to stem from fundamental cognitive differences, since empathy requires context sensitivity (Vermeulen 2012), and since the social world is not governed by clear, fixed rules (Baron-Cohen 2011). Finally, the highly repetitive behaviours of autistic individuals are explained by appeal to a highly systematic processing style (Baron-Cohen 2011). Clearly, this apparent explanatory power is part of the appeal of the cognitivist accounts.

The second strength of the cognitivist accounts concerns their utility. In general, the classification of autism has lacked utility from a medical per spective (Hassal 2017). But the cognitivist accounts have had some limited success, through the development of empowering and non-invasive educational techniques based on specific theories of autistic cognition. One example, based on the hyper-systemising theory of autism, is an interac tive DVD that helps autistic people learn to understand emotions via facial expressions (Baron-Cohen 2003; Golan and Baron-Cohen 2006). On the DVD, six actors each display 412 distinctly labelled expressions, allowing the viewer to memorise these and then systematically map them on the world in order to better function within it. A different example, based on the context-blindness theory of autism, aims to teach the Socratic method to autistic children so as to help them learn to deal with conceptual prob lems, grasp vague concepts, and improve their general functioning and mode of life (Vermeulen 2013). This method is taken to work because it uses reflective, logical thinking in order to understand complicated cate gories and concepts that require strong context sensitivity in order to be understood spontaneously (Vermeulen 2012). In each case, these two otherwise quite different approaches to improving autistic lives are based on the premise that characteristically autistic problems can be dealt with from 利用与孤独谱系相关的认知优势来克服与孤独谱系认知局限相关的问题，从而由内 而外地加以解决。

鉴于其解释力以及在提供看似赋能的技巧方面的实用性，认知主义框架一直主导着孤独谱系研究。然而，这种方法至少遇到了两个主要问题。首先，关于孤独谱系神经与认知功能的研究发现了广泛的潜在多重因果性和异质性。虽然确实可能存在对某些认知和神经特征的非常普遍的统计倾向，但尚未发现所有或大多数孤独谱系群体共有的核心特质，也没有发现能够解释所有典型孤独谱系特征的特质（Waterhouse2013; Timimi, Gardner, and McCabe 2010）。更糟糕的是，主流的认知主义方法不仅假设了不同的本质特征：有些还假设了相互对立、矛盾的本质特征。例如，近期一种理论将过度同理心而非不足同理心视为核心特征（Makram, Rinaldi, and Makram 2007）。鉴于这些种种担忧，一些领先的认知主义者建议完全放弃统一孤独谱系的概念（Happé and Frith 2006）。而另一些则进一步提出，孤独谱系概念缺乏科学有效性，应予以摒弃（Waterhouse 2013; Timimi, Gardner,and McCabe 2010）。

或许我们可以接受一种更模糊、集群式的孤独谱系认知特征概念，以避免导致第一类批评的内在本质主义。但第二个问题仍然存在。这关乎孤独谱系的认知主义理解似乎未能充分考虑那些部分构成孤独谱系思维的具体化、互动性、关系性及发展过程（Hobson 2002; Gallagher2004）。在这种观点下，即使核心认知特征对于理解孤独谱系思维至关重要，神经认知论框架却忽略了其他同样甚至更为关键的因素。在此我不对第一类批评做任何补充。我更感兴趣的是，近年来出现了摒弃上述第二类批评中认知主义还原论的孤独谱系替代解释。我将自己的贡献置于这一反传统之中，并将在后续论述中朝此方向展开；就本文目的而言，神经认知主义自可自圆其说。

Given its explanatory power and utility in providing apparently empowering techniques, the cognitivist framework has dominated autism studies. Still, this approach has encountered at least two major problems. First, the research on autistic neurological and cognitive functioning has found a vast underlying multi-causality and heterogeneity. While i is true that there may be very general statistical tendencies towards cer tain cognitive and neurological characteristics, there is no core trait or traits that have been found to be shared by all or most autistic people, or that can explain all characteristically autistic traits (Waterhouse 2013; Timimi, Gardner, and McCabe 2010). To make matters worse, dominant cognitivist approaches do not just posit different essential traits: some have posited opposed, contradictory essential traits. For instance, one recent theory posits hyper-empathy, rather than hypo-empathy, as a core trait (Makram, Rinaldi, and Makram 2007). Given these various worries, some leading cognitivists have suggested giving up on the notion of a unified autism altogether (Happé and Frith 2006). And others have gone so far as to suggest that the concept of autism lacks scientific validity, and should thus be abandoned (Waterhouse 2013; Timimi, Gardner, and McCabe 2010).

It may be that we can accept a fuzzier, cluster conception of autistic cognitive traits in order to avoid the implicit essentialism that leads to the first set of criticisms. But still a second issue remains. This concerns how the cognitivist understanding of autism seems to fail to take into account embodied, interactive, relational, and developmental processes that are partly constitutive of autistic thinking (Hobson 2002; Gallagher 2004). On this view, even if core cognitive traits are important for understanding autistic thinking, the neuro-cognitivist framework overlooks other factors that are equally, perhaps more, vital. I shall not add anything to the first set of criticisms here. I am more interested in how recent years have seen the emergence of alternative accounts of autism that spurn the cognitivist reductionism just noted in this second line of criticism. I situate my own contribution in this counter-tradition and shall address the remainder of my remarks in that direction; for my purposes here, neuro-cognitivism can take care of itself.

## Hobson’s Wittgensteinian Account

In opposition to the neuro-cognitivist framing, a number of psycholo gists and philosophers—most notably Peter Hobson—have drawn on Wittgenstein as well as the phenomenological tradition to argue that the cognitivist approach misframes and oversimplifies both the nature of autism and its associated problems (see especially Hobson 2009 but also 1993, 2002, 2013; Montgomery 1997; Gallagher 2004; Tomasello et al.

2005）。我在此重点讨论Hobson的论述，因为它是现有观点中最具影响力和最全面发展的。

一方面，Hobson赞同认知主义者的观点，即孤独谱系通常源于各种先天差异。然而，他既否定了神经认知核心的概念，也否定了孤独谱系必须源于认知特质这一说法。相反，他认为关键的孤独谱系差异更多地与孤独谱系婴儿的价值取向有关，这些婴儿难以与他人建立情感联系和互动，因此关键在于他们难以进入一个由主体间意义构成的“共享世界”（Hobson 2009）。换言之，该主张认为孤独谱系差异位于主体间层面，而非个体认知的主观层面，尽管在大多数个体案例中其因果起源往往在于后者（Tomasello 等2005年）。重要的是，此外，虽然Hobson承认孤独谱系差异通常源于与神经典型人群的神经认知差异，但他强调这些差异在每个案例中可能是独特的。此外，他认为孤独谱系差异同样可能源于先天性失明（Hobson 2014），甚至（更罕见地）源于极端的忽视或虐待案例（Hobson 2002）。

在这种观点看来，孤独谱系在理解上的问题与其说是与静态认知差异相关的持续处理失败，不如说是未能恰当地与人类共享生活形式——进而与人类语言和意义的规则相协调所产生的下游效应。当我们考虑神经学和认知方面的潜在异质性与多重因果性时，这一框架相比于主流的神经认知主义解释具有某些优势。孤独谱系具有关系本质——即无法协调于人类意义的共享世界——这一概念与存在大量、不可穷尽的认知、神经学、环境等方面的因果相关变异是完全兼容的。类比而言，认知主义者将孤独谱系心智视为具有特异处理器的计算机，而根据这一解释，孤独谱系心智更像是未能连接互联网，从而在发展中丢失了许多重要的软件更新（Tantam 2009）。正如未能连接互联网可能源于各种潜在的物理或处理原因一样，孤独谱系个体缺乏人际关联也可能源于无数种原因，这些原因却导致了相似的发展效应。根据Hobson的观点，这向我们揭示了孤独谱系的真实本质。孤独谱系个体的思维并非由于静态的认知局限而受阻，而是因为其缺乏主体间性的语言与意义共享世界所具有的结构性而受到扰乱。

On the one hand, Hobson agrees with the cognitivists that autism typically stems from various innate differences. He rejects, however, both the notion of a neurocognitive core and that autism must necessarily stem from cognitive traits at all. Instead, he suggests that the key autistic differences are more axiological to autistic infants who struggle to emotionally connect and interact with others and thus, crucially, struggle to tap into a “shared world” of intersubjective meaning (Hobson 2009). In other words, the claim is that autistic difference is located at the inter-subjective level, rather than the subjective level of individual cognition, even if its causal genesis often lies in the latter in most individual cases (Tomasello et al. 2005). Importantly, and in addition, while Hobson admits that autis tic difference typically stems from neuro-cognitive differences to the neuro-typical population, he stresses that these differences may be unique in each case. Moreover, he holds that autistic difference can equally stem from congenital blindness (Hobson 2014) or even (more rarely) extreme cases of neglect or abuse (Hobson 2002).

On this view, autistic problems in understanding are not so much a matter of constant processing failures relating to static cognitive differences as the downstream effect of not being properly attuned to humanity’s shared form of life, and thus humanity’s rules of language and meaning. When we consider the underlying heterogeneity and multi-causality in terms of neurology and cognition, this framework has certain advantages over dominant neuro-cognitivist accounts. The notion that autism has a relational essence—namely, the inability to attune to the shared world of human meaning—is perfectly compatible with there being a vast, indefinite number of causally relevant variants in terms of cognition, neurology, environment, and so forth. By analogy, rather than seeing autistic minds as computers with an idiosyncratic processor, as the cognitivists do, on this account it is more that autistic minds fail to connect to the internet and thus lose many important software updates as they develop (Tantam 2009). And just as a failure to connect to the internet can stem from a variety of underlying physical or processing causes, so too can an autistic lack of interpersonal relatedness stem from a countless variety of causes that nonetheless lead to similar developmental effects. According to Hobson, this shows us the true nature of autism. Rather than being a matter of static cognitive limitation, the thinking of autistic individuals is disrupted due to its being less structured than the intersubjective shared world of language and meaning.

## The Double Empathy Problem

It might seem at this point that the cognitivists are too focused on the subjective, while the Wittgensteinians are too focused on the intersub jective. If the issue is one of emphasis, then combining the two accounts together might allow a less-reductionistic middle ground. After all, it is not unthinkable (from either perspective) that autistic people both have different operating systems and fail to connect to the internet. Unfortunately, however, things are not this simple. For there is another issue that further undermines both accounts. This problem, initially proposed by Ian Hacking (2009; 2010) and further developed by autistic academics Damian Milton (2012) and Nicholas Chown (2014), has come to be called the “double empathy problem.” It consists of two parts. Together, they disrupt the notion that autistic people simply lack a ca pacity for empathy or interpersonal relatedness when compared with the purportedly functional neurotypical.

The first aspect of the double empathy problem is less relevant here but will be worth noting briefly. This aspect concerns how issues in empathising, rather than simply coming from the autistic side, actually go both ways between the autistic and neurotypical populations. As Victoria McGeer nicely summarises, writing from a neurotypical perspective: “They are blind to our minds, but so too are we blind to theirs” (2010, 286). Recent empirical research has given this some support (Edey et al. 2016; Sasson et al. 2017). Building on this, the second aspect of the double empathy problem concerns the fact that autistic individuals often do seem to be able to intersub jectively attune to other autistic individuals. As one autistic person writes: “If I socialize with other Aspergians of pretty much my own functionality, then all of the so-called social impairments simply don’t exist . . . we share the same operating systems, so there are no impairments” (Cornish 2008, 158). 这一点至关重要，因为一旦我们考虑到双向同理心问题，孤独谱系调谐和共情方面的问题似乎并非单纯存在于孤独谱系人群中的固有缺陷，而更多是个体与社区之间的不匹配。这对Hobson关于孤独谱系群体存在固有关系缺陷的观点（因为至少部分孤独谱系者实际上能够与同类建立关系）以及神经认知主义者关于孤独谱系群体相对于神经典型规范存在固有同理心缺陷的观点（因为孤独谱系者和神经典型群体都能对同类产生共情）都构成了重大挑战。因此，要更好地理解孤独谱系，我们不能简单地将认知主义解释与Hobson等维特根斯坦学派的观点合并。两者不仅在侧重点上（即过于由内而外或过于由外而内）存在错误，更根本的是，它们各自都误解了孤独谱系及其相关问题本质中的某些关键点。

Importantly, the experiences described here from both sides seem to be an exact reverse of what we see when autistic individuals reside in neu rotypical space, as is usually the case. That is, in autistic space, autistic people often do seem to be attuned to those around them and experience the various benefits that come with this; while conversely, neurotypicals seemingly experience all the characteristic problems and anxieties associated with a lack of empathy or intersubjective attunement—problems typically thought of as essentially autistic.

This is relevant since, once we take the double empathy problem into account, it seems the problems in autistic attunement and empathising are not a matter of an inherent deficit found simply in the autistic population but are more a matter of mismatch between the individual and the com munity. This presents a significant problem for Hobson’s notion that autistic people have an inherent relational deficit (since at least some autistic people can, in fact, relate to people like them) as well as for the neuro-cog nitivist notion that autistic people have an inherent empathy deficiency in relation to a neurotypical norm (for both autistics and neurotypicals can empathise with their own kind). So in order to better understand autism, we cannot simply combine the claims of the cognitivist accounts with those of the Wittgensteinians such as Hobson. For both seem to be wrong not just in terms of emphasis (that is, too inside out or outside in); rather, each has fundamentally misunderstood something crucial about the very nature of autism, and its associated problems, as such.

Nonetheless, there is an alternative Wittgensteinian framing that may help us account for the double empathy problem. For as McGeer (2010) points out, on a Wittgensteinian framing, rather than there being a single human form of life that we can either stand inside or outside (through cognitive or relational deficit), we can instead say that there are two or more human forms of life, or perhaps that the human form of life is com plex enough to have several subsets.<sup>3</sup> For this would precisely leave room for members of each kind being more able to attune to other members of their own kind, and yet remain less able to attune to members of the other, without needing to make the further claim—which, as we have seen, is unsustainable—that one side or another simply lacks the capacity for empathy and interpersonal relatedness as such.

From the outside in, then, McGeer, along with Hacking, hypothesises that autism is a different form of life, which, due to complex social-histor ical circumstances, is only just beginning to emerge as having its own cul ture and language. That is, autistic people are beginning to connect with each other, largely through the internet, in order to cultivate the space for shared autistic understanding for those with this form of life. In Hacking’s 换言之，孤独谱系社群“正在发展一种语言，或者，如果你愿意，可以说是 一种新的语言游戏，它就在我们眼前和耳畔被创造出来。这种言语又反过来 创造或延伸了一种方式，让那些非常特殊的人——即孤独谱系者——得以存 在、得以生活”（2010，262–63）。这一点至关重要。因为在此之前，正 如孤独谱系自我倡权者Amanda Baggs所写，正如“计数器、楼梯和饮水 机是由健全人建造的”一样，“语言也大多由非孤独谱系群体构建，结果显 而易见，而我最感挫败的是：关于我感知和与周围世界互动的最重要方式， 只能用描述它们缺失某种重要事物的术语来表达”（Baggs 2010）。

Baggs的观点或许能帮助我们理解Hobson和认知主义者究竟错在哪里。在他们看来，孤独谱系群体只能被理解为有所缺失，是在人类生命形式或人类认知方面存在缺陷。然而，Baggs却用不同的交流方式反驳了这一点——她指出，这些交流方式通常只有其他孤独谱系群体才能理解，而且对她而言，“它们本身就是丰富多样的交流形式，而非更标准交流形式的拙劣替代品”（2010）。除非我们直接否定Baggs及无数其他孤独谱系自我倡权者的主张，否则他们的证词更符合不同生命形式假说，而非认为孤独谱系群体不过是神经典型生命形式之缺陷版本的观点。因为根据生活形式假说，由于神经典型群体难以共情孤独谱系个体，后者在神经典型视角下，正如McGeer所写，“必然显得⋯⋯仿佛他们并不具备复杂或发达的心智品质”（2010, 287）。但生活形式假说提供了一种不同的理解方式，来解释为何孤独谱系个体在神经典型群体眼中会呈现如此面貌。

## 相互面相盲

我刚刚提到，认知主义方法和Hobson的维特根斯坦式论述都错误地认为，孤独谱系必须被理解为相对于神经典型规范存在缺陷，并指出将孤独谱系视为一种不同生活形式的概念可能揭示这种假设如何导致这些论述的问题。但不仅如此，我认为，我们可以通过考察维特根斯坦所谓的“方面盲”（1953, § 193）来进一步解释双向同理心问题，而无需假设孤独谱系人群存在缺陷。<sup>4</sup> 对于维特根斯坦而言，方面盲指的是当某人具有一定程度的理解但仍遗漏某些方面的情况。例如，观察

This speech is, in turn, creating or extending a way for very unusual people— namely, autistic ones—to be, to exist, to live” (2010, 262–63). This is important. For until now, as autistic self-advocate Amanda Baggs writes, just as “counters, stairs, and drinking fountains [are built by able-bodied people], language was built mostly by non-autistic people, with the obvious results, and my biggest frustration is this: the most important things about the way I perceive and interact with the world around me can only be expressed in terms that describe them as the absence of something important” (Baggs 2010).

Baggs’s point may help us understand where both Hobson and the cognitivists go wrong. To them, autistic people can only be understood as having something missing, as being deficient in relation to the human form of life, or human cognition, as such. And yet against this Baggs uses different forms of communicating that, she notes, are usually understood only by other autistic people, and that, for her, “are rich and varied forms of communication in their own right, not inadequate substitutes for the more standard forms of communication” (2010). Unless we simply dismiss the claims made by Baggs and countless other autistic self-advocates, their testimony fits more easily with the different-form-of-life hypothesis than with the notion that autistic people are simply a deficient version of the neurotypical form of life. For on the form-of-life hypothesis, since neurotypicals struggle to empathise with autistic individuals, the latter, writes McGeer, “are bound to seem . . . as if they do not have complex or well developed qualities of mind” (2010, 287), when viewed from a neurotypical perspective. But the form-of-life hypothesis allows for a different way of understanding why autistic individuals seem like this to neurotypicals.

## Mutual Aspect-Blindness

I have just suggested that both the cognitivist approach and Hobson’s Wittgensteinian account wrongly suppose that autism must be understood as deficient in relation to a neurotypical norm, and suggested that the notion of autism as a different form of life may show how this suppo sition leads to issues with these accounts. But more than this, I think, we can further account for the double empathy problem without the assumption that the autistic population is deficient, by considering what Wittgenstein called “aspect-blindness” (1953, § 193).<sup>4</sup> For Wittgenstein, aspect-blindness refers to whenever someone has some level of under standing but nonetheless misses certain aspects. 相互面相盲这一概念至关重要，因为它能够让我们从内部出发理解孤独谱系体验，其方式与神经认知主义解释类似。然而，与这些解释不同的是，它还具有额外的价值，即能够以与双向同理心问题一致的方式来构建这一框架。因为在神经认知主义框架下，孤独谱系群体对社会世界的任何误解都可能被归结为孤独谱系的“语境盲”或“心智盲”，并与假设的神经典型群体的“语境敏感性”或“读心”能力相对比。然而，由于两种群体的生活形式不同，双方都存在方面盲的替代性概念反而有助于解释和说明这两个群体之间的相同现象，而非将孤独谱系群体简单地视为存在处理缺陷。因为正如McGeer所指出的，从维特根斯坦视角来看，社会理解是“一种技能性感知，它产生于任何规范支配的共同实践或生活形式中的熟练表现”（2010，286）。因此，如果一个人在与他交流的另一个人所共享的世界或生活形式中没有充分协调一致，那么正是在缺乏这种引导性语境的情况下，双方在试图理解对方时都会陷入方面盲。简而言之，正如维特根斯坦的名言：“如果狮子能说话，我们也无法理解它”（1953，§223），因为我们生活形式上的差异会让我们对其话语的含义严重方面盲。当然，这里的鸿沟远没有人类与狮子之间的那么大一—每个群体都能在相当程度上理解对方——但至少仍然存在显著的方面盲倾向。<sup>5</sup> 因此，维特根斯坦框架能够像认知主义解释那样解释孤独谱系群体对社会世界的主观体验，然而它

A person is also aspect-blind when she en counters similar problems with regard to understanding facial expres sions or figurative language, as well as with picking up on humour and irony. Notably, of course, these are the precise problems that arise be tween the autistic and neurotypical populations: being blind to at least aspects of expressive bodily movements, utterances, and so on, when ex pressed by the other population, seems to be the core problem that arises when the divide is crossed.

The notion of mutual aspect-blindness is important, as it can allow us to understand autistic experience from the inside out, in a way similar to the neuro-cognitivist accounts. Unlike those accounts, however, it has the added value of having the space to frame this in a way that is consistent with the double empathy problem. For on the neuro-cognitivist framing, any autistic misunderstanding of the social world might be framed in terms of, say, autistic “context blindness” or “mind blindness,” which is compared to an assumed neurotypical “context sensitivity” or capacity to “mind read.” Nonetheless, the alternative notion of aspect-blindness in both groups, due to their different forms of life, can help account for and explain the same phenomena between the two populations, rather than framing autistic people as simply having faulty processing. For, as McGeer notes, from a Wittgensteinian perspective, social understanding is “a spe cies of skilled perception that arises in conjunction with skilled perfor mance in any norm-governed shared practice, or form of life” (2010, 286). Hence, if one individual is not sufficiently attuned to the shared world, or part of the form of life, of another individual he is communicating with then it is precisely in the absence of this guiding context that each will be aspect-blind when trying to understand the other. In short, as Wittgenstein famously wrote, “If a lion could speak, we would not understand him” (1953, § 223), since the differences in our forms of life would make us heav ily aspect-blind to the meaning of its words. Of course, the divide here is not nearly as great as one between humans and lions—each population can understand the other to a considerable extent—but, still, there is a significant tendency towards aspect-blindness at least.<sup>5</sup> Hence, the Wittgensteinian framework has room for accounting for autistic subjective experience of the social world in the way the cognitivist accounts do, yet i 还具有额外的优势，即能够以充分解释双向同理心问题的方式做到这一 点，而不是错误地假设一种缺陷论解释。

## 系统化再探

我已开始勾勒关于孤独谱系心智本质的一般性描述，使其与生活形式假说相契合。然而，就目前的发展而言，该描述仅能容纳与双向同理心问题相关的现象，却无法解释孤独谱系思维的其他方面。尽管所有孤独谱系个体并不共享某种本质性的认知特质，但在实证研究中最受支持的孤独谱系倾向，便是那种高度机械化、系统化的思维方式（Greenberg等 2018）。幸运的是，我认为维特根斯坦在此也能提供帮助。与此相关的，是他关于不确定性和焦虑的思考——当人们迷失在他所谓的古老却不断生长的意义与语言“城市”中时（1953, § 18），这种不确定性和焦虑便可能浮现。

在大多数情况下，对维特根斯坦而言，人类确定性——即我们在与世界互动中展现的前认知信任——是由大量语境所维系的。关于人类发展，他写道：“儿童学会了相信大量事物。也就是说，它学会根据这些信念来行动。渐渐地，形成了一个信念系统，在这个系统中，有些东西坚不可摧地固定着，有些东西则或多或少容易变动。固定不变的东西之所以固定，并非因为它本身显而易见或令人信服；而是被周围的东西所固定。”（1969, § 144）。换言之，我们的知识以及我们底层的前认知信任，都是由更广泛的共享信念与实践网络所维系的。而这个信念系统，通过人类惯例与共享生活形式中的一致所构筑的发展心智，得以不断成长。因此，根据这一描述，对自发的、前认知的确信——如何回应、说什么、面部表情意味着什么等等——并非准确处理的问题，而是基于我们成长过程中融入的共享世界——即我们成为其一部分的生活形式——的背景来处理的问题。

相比之下，当我们考虑一个在陌生生活形式中发展的人的情况时，我们恰恰会预期她缺乏直觉知识和前认知信任的某些方面。因为对于一个仅与主导信念系统半调谐的个体而言，她并不因此缺乏稳固的知识基础：我们恰恰应当预期，困惑、概念怀疑论和前认知焦虑会持续在日常中出现。对世界的持续直觉理解依赖于深层沉浸在一种生活形式之中——has the added benefit of doing so in such a way that it can adequatelyaccount for the double empathy problem instead of wrongly supposing adeficit-based account.

## Systemising Revisited

I have begun to outline a general account of the nature of the autistic mind as it would fit with the form-of-life hypothesis. Nonetheless, as I have developed it so far, this account has room only for phenomena associated with the double empathy problem. It does not help explain other aspects of autistic thinking. Although there is no essential cogni tive trait shared by all autistic individuals, the autistic tendency with the most empirical support is that which has highly mechanistic, systematic thinking (Greenberg et al. 2018). Happily, I posit, Wittgenstein can also help here. Of particular relevance in this regard are his considerations regarding uncertainty and angst that can arise in light of getting lost in, as he puts it, the ancient, yet always growing, “city” of meaning and language (1953, § 18).

In most cases, for Wittgenstein, human certainty—that is, our preepistemic trust that manifests in our engagements with the world—is held in place by a great deal of context. With regard to human development, he writes: “The child learns to believe a host of things. I.e. it learns to act according to these beliefs. Bit by bit there forms a system of what is believed, and in that system some things stand unshakably fast and some things are more or less liable to shift. What stands fast does so, not because it is intrinsically obvious or convincing; it is rather held fast by what lies around it” (1969, § 144). Both our knowing and our underlying pre-epistemic trust, in other words, are held in place by a wider nexus of shared beliefs and practices. And this belief system grows through the constitu tion of our developing minds by the scaffolding of human convention and agreements in shared forms of life. So, on this account, access to spontaneous pre-epistemic surety—how to respond, what to say, what facial expressions mean, and so forth—is not so much a matter of accurate processing as of processing against the background of the shared world that we become part of as we grow and interact with other members of our form of life.

By contrast, however, when we consider the case of someone who devel ops within an alien form of life, as it were, then we would precisely expect her to lack both certain aspects of intuitive knowledge and pre-epistemic trust. For the individual who develops only half-attuned to the dominant system of belief would thereby not lack such a secure foundation for her knowledge: confusion, conceptual scepticism, and pre-epistemic anxiety would, we should precisely expect, continually arise in day-to-day life. Initially, on Wittgenstein’s analysis, when pre-epistemic uncertainty or conceptual confusion arises, we can experience what he describes as angst or disquietude. And in light of being gripped by this, he further notes, it is natural to be overcome by a craving for generality that compels us towards over-simplifying, generalising, and searching for rigid essences that we think will help us cure us of the angst that arises when certainty or understanding breaks down (1958, 17–19). Similarly, as the clinician Tony Atwood writes regarding his autistic patients, “Being anxious will affect the person’s thinking and lead to the development of strategies to reduce the level of anxiety. . . . When a person with Asperger’s syndrome is anxious, his or her thinking tends to become more rigid” (2007, 137). On the form-of-life account this response is not in itself an essentially autistic one but rather one that all humans are prone to in light of pre-epistemic angst; the difference, as we have seen, is just that autistic people who develop in neurotypical space will be much more prone to experiencing this, compared with those who dwell with their own kind.<sup>6</sup>

To summarise, I have suggested here that a core feature of autistic thinking—the tendency to be highly systematic—is not so much a static, computer-like cognitive difference as a very human reaction to developing in a different form of life. This would account for how there seems to be a strong tendency towards systemising among the autistic population despite underlying neurological heterogeneity: hyper-systemising is more social than biological. But there are also, it must be acknowledged, many other cognitive traits that are sometimes associated with autism, ranging from memory-processing differences to sensory-integration differences, which I have not discussed. Is my position also able to account for these features of autism? I do not want to claim that all these various traits can be accounted for socially, in the way I have accounted for the double empathy problem and autistic systemising tendencies. Notably, however, the form-of-life hypothesis is not committed to a rejection of the claim that autism also involves biologically based traits, just that it can be reduced to 单一的缺陷或静态的神经认知本质。事实上，正是因为生活形式假说认 为孤独谱系涉及一组相互关联的特征，它才承认孤独谱系人群在具体表 现何种特征上具有多样性。当然，要厘清孤独谱系人群中可能存在的各 种特征之间众多不同的关联，需要付出大量细致的工作。但理解这些特 征如何相互关联，更像是梳理家族成员之间的相似联系，而非寻找一个 所有成员共有的特征。因此，尽管我在此所言不多，但这些进一步的特 征也与生活形式假说相吻合。

## 启示

我在此尝试给出我认为首个关于孤独谱系心智的全面解释，该解释同时说明了双向同理心问题、孤独谱系系统化和孤独谱系的异质性——这三个核心的、经验证实的特征，它们此前似乎无法调和。如果我的论证始终具有说服力，那么生活形式假说似乎是整合我们目前所知一切的最连贯的方式。但这个解释不仅在解释力方面有帮助。它也有伦理和实践上的启示。最后我将指出我提出的理解孤独谱系方式的三个重要启示，然后回答一个可能的担忧。

首先，在我看来，我的解释的一个优点是，它能够承认孤独谱系群体的差异，而不假定他们在神经典型规范方面存在缺陷。通过承认孤独谱系群体与神经典型群体共享不同的生活形式，我们也可以允许更多的相似性显现出来——将孤独谱系人群坚定地带回人类大家庭。也就是说，虽然神经认知主义解释往往将孤独谱系个体描绘为有些机器人化从而非人化，而霍布森解释则明确地将孤独谱系个体置于人类生命形式之外，但我的解释并不暗示孤独谱系个体被如此他者化。讽刺的是，只有在认识到孤独谱系生活形式的差异时，我们才能看到孤独谱系群体和神经典型群体之间更重要的相似性，从而促进对彼此人性的更深入理解。

第二个重要启示在于，对于维特根斯坦而言，我刚刚讨论的这类问题与其说是认知缺陷或医学意义上的智力残障，不如说是人类面对前认识论焦虑时非常自然的反应。更具体地说，这些问题源于被某种思维方式及与之对应的语言“图像”所误导的结果一一我们以为这种图像能平息焦虑，但实际上却往往加剧了我们反复陷入类似问题的倾向。

Indeed, precisely because the form-of-life hypothesis is committed to a view of autism as involving a cluster of related features, it is open to the possibility that the autistic population is diverse in terms of which features are manifest. Of course, it would take a great deal of careful work to draw out the many and different connections between the various features that may be present in autistic populations. Hence, although I have less to add here, these further features also cohere with the form-of-life hypothesis.

## Implications

I have attempted here to give what I think is the first general account of the autistic mind that simultaneously accounts for the double empathy problem, autistic systemising, and autism’s heterogeneity—three core, empirically established features that together previously seemed irrecon cilable. If my argument throughout has been convincing, the form-of-life hypothesis seems the most coherent way of bringing together everything we currently know. But this account is not just helpful in terms of its ex planatory power. It has ethical and practical implications too. I shall end by noting three important implications of my suggested way of framing autism, and then answer one potential worry.

First, on my view, a strength of my account is that it manages to acknowledge autistic people as being different without the assumption that they are deficient in relation to a neurotypical norm. By acknowledg ing autistic people as sharing a form of life different to that of neurotypi cals, we can also allow more similarities to manifest themselves—bringing the autistic population firmly back into the fold of humanity. That is, while neuro-cognitivist accounts tend to represent autistic individuals as somewhat robotic and thereby inhuman, and the Hobsonian account explicitly posits autistic individuals as outside the human form of life, my account does not have the implication that autistic individuals are so oth ered. Ironically, it is only in recognising the difference of the autistic form of life that we can come to see even more important similarities between the autistic and neurotypical populations, allowing for a greater shared understanding of the humanity of each.

A second important implication regards how the kinds of problems Ihave just discussed are, for Wittgenstein, not so much a matter of faultycognition, or intellectual disability in a medical sense, but rather a veryhuman reaction to pre-epistemic angst. More specifically, these problemsare reactions to having been misled by a certain manner of thinking and acorrelating “picture” of language, which we think will help quell our anxieties but which in fact tend to reinforce our tendency to run into similar问题，周而复始。事实上，Gerland 恰恰以这种方式详细阐述了自己系统化的倾向及其伴随的问题：“我拼命想要理解，这催生了许多理论[但]有时会出现例外，让我怀疑自己的理论。那种怀疑是一种痛苦的感觉⋯⋯我就像一具被理论之线操控的精神提线木偶。当理论不再适用，而我又找不到新的东西可以依靠时，我就无法在精神上移动。所以我必须不停地创造新理论”（2003, 26–27）。在这里，我们清楚地看到，在缺乏自发理解和确定性的情况下，她持续尝试进行系统化。然而，这却让 Gerland 不断寻求越来越精确的定义和系统，因为她的理论不可避免地会撞上人类意义世界的流动。重要的是，在外界看来，她似乎只是缺乏理解能力本身，这更符合医学上智力残障的概念。但实际上，令她痛苦和残障的，更多是成长于一种足够不同的生活形式所伴随的焦虑与不确定性，以及她在应对这些问题时所采用的那种加剧问题而非解决问题的办法。这为我们理解孤独谱系个体常遇到的问题开辟了一条新路：这些问题并非仅仅是持续存在的智力缺陷，而是在这类焦虑出现时人类不可避免的普遍倾向。

第三个含义更为实际，且在一定程度上与之前提到的神经认知主义者试图增强孤独谱系理解的努力一致，例如教孤独谱系儿童苏格拉底方法，或帮助他们系统化通用面部表情。在这方面，在我看来最重要的是，维特根斯坦不仅帮助我们诊断我们与孤独谱系相关的各种问题，他还提供了解决这些问题的途径。因为根据他对僵化倾向的分析——他认为这些倾向源于前认识论焦虑和不确定性——他提出了多种实用方法和建议，供那些在我们一直讨论的各种方式中感到迷失或不确定的人使用。虽然我在此没有篇幅深入探讨这些，但至关重要的是、在其他地方探索利用维特根斯坦的澄清伦理学来帮助孤独谱系个体茁壮成长的潜力。将孤独谱系重新纳入人类大家庭，同时承认孤独谱系与神经典型不同，也具有伦理意义。因为它开启了谈论孤独谱系特定伦理学的空间，其目标是通过孤独谱系实现繁荣，而非通过旨在治疗孤独谱系从而消除差异的医学治疗。<sup>7</sup>

Indeed, Gerland clearly elaborates on her tendency to systemise, and its correlating problems, in exactly this way: “I desperately wanted to understand, and this led to theories [but] there were sometimes exceptions that made me doubt my theory. That doubt was a painful feeling, and . . . I was a mental marionette hanging on strings of theories. When theories didn’t fit and I could find nothing new to hang on, I was unable to move mentally. So I had to create new theories all the time” (2003, 26–27). Here we see precisely a continued attempt to systemise in light of a lack of spontaneous understanding and certainty. And yet this leaves Gerland always seeking more and more precise definitions and systems, as her theories inevitably crash against the flux of human meaning. Importantly, to the outside world, she simply seemed to lack the capacity to understand as such, more in line with a medical conception of intel lectual disability. But in fact she was distressed and disabled more by the angst and uncertainty that come from growing up in a sufficiently different form of life, and by trying to deal with these problems in a way that amplified rather than helped. This opens up a new way of understanding the kinds of problems autistic individuals tend to face as very human tenden cies that emerge whenever such angst arises, rather than a mere persisting intellectual deficit.

The third implication is more practical and somewhat in line with the neuro-cognitivist attempts to enhance autistic understanding mentioned earlier, such as teaching autistic children the Socratic method or helping them to systemise generic facial expressions. In this regard, what seems most relevant to me is that Wittgenstein does not just help us to diagnose the kinds of problems we associate with autism; he also offers a way out of them. For in light of his analysis of the rigid tendencies that he believed arise from pre-epistemic angst and uncertainty, he developed a variety of practical methods and advice for those who found themselves lost or uncertain in the various ways we have been considering. Although I do not have the space to go into these here, it is imperative that the potential for using Wittgenstein’s clarificatory ethics to help autistic individuals to thrive be explored elsewhere. Bringing autism back into the fold of humanity whilst simultaneously recognising autism as different to the neurotypical has eth ical significance too. For it opens up the space to speak of autism-specific ethics, with the aim being thriving through autism, rather than medical therapy aimed at treating autism so that the difference is erased.<sup>7</sup>

I have just given some reason for optimism. But to end, it is important to see off a set of related potential worries relating to my way offraming of autism as different rather than deficient. On the one hand,my proposed way of viewing autism as a difference might, to some, seem可能忽视了与孤独谱系相关的伤害。毕竟，任何被归类为精神障碍的人类种类，通常被认为不仅在功能上存在缺陷，而且在定义上就是有害的（Bolton 2008）。而孤独谱系本身确实与较低的福祉相关。我们会不会最终不当地忽视了这种伤害？也许我甚至面临着滑入一种伦理上可疑的相对主义的危险？事实上，这里还有一个相关的潜在担忧：如果我们接受不同生命形式假说，那么我们就可能更难以何种标准来区分不同生命形式与真正的病症。例如，精神病态——通常也与同理心缺陷相关——仅仅是一种不同的生命形式吗？我们该在哪里划清界限？

本文无法一劳永逸地解决这些担忧及其相关的争议。但至少有两件事值得一提。首先，我乐于接受这样的观点：将内在倾向导致临床水平的伤害——且该伤害主要源于内部功能障碍而非偶然社会过程——视为区分病症与单纯差异的有用方法。同时，我也乐于承认孤独谱系个体目前确实容易面临临床水平的伤害。我所质疑的是，这种伤害是否源于孤独谱系个体神经认知能力中固有的任何因素。在此，我遵循近年来的传统，认为孤独谱系人群所面临的伤害主要由偶然社会因素（如污名和障碍性壁垒）造成（Robertson 2010）。我最近在其他地方论证过，有多种理由支持这种替代性框架（Chapman 2019）。本文提出的论点当然无法彻底解决这一争议，但可视为对这些理由的补充。其次，虽然生活形式假说是否能应用于其他精神病症类型有待进一步探讨，但不应认为它可以自动适用于任何情况。毕竟，不同病症相关的特质和伤害在每种情况下都是独特的。例如，尽管孤独谱系和精神病态常被归为同理心障碍，但近年来已形成近乎共识的观点：两者相关的同理心问题存在重要差异（参见 Baron‑Cohen 2011; Bollard 2013）。因此，即使某些案例最初看似具有重要的相似性，也不应认为针对单一案例的结论可以自动适用于其他案例。

After all, any human kind classified as a mental disorder is usually taken to be not just func tionally deficient but harmful by definition (Bolton 2008). And autism itself is certainly associated with lowered well-being. Might we end up unduly overlooking the harm? Perhaps I am even in danger of sliding into an ethically dubious form of relativism? Indeed, a related possible worry here is that if we accept the different form of life hypothesis, then it may become less clear on what grounds we could distinguish differen forms of life from genuine disorders across the board. Is, say, psychopa thy—which is also typically associated with empathy deficits—simply a different form of life too? Where would we draw the line?

It is beyond the scope of this paper to settle these worries and the related disputes surrounding them once and for all. But it is worth saying two things at least. First, I am happy to accept the notion that an inherent disposition towards clinical levels of harm—where the harm is taken to stem primarily from an internal dysfunction rather than from contingent social processes—is a helpful way of distinguishing disor der from mere difference. And I am also happy to accept that autistic individuals do currently tend to face clinical levels of harm. What I contest is that this harm stems from anything inherent in the neurocognitive capacities of autistic individuals. Here I follow a recent tradition of taking the harms faced by the autistic population to be primarily caused by contingent social factors, such as stigma and disabling barri ers (Robertson 2010). I have recently argued elsewhere that there are a number of reasons to favour this alternative framing (Chapman 2019) The case made in the present article will surely not settle the dispute; but it can be taken as adding to these reasons. Second, while the ques tion of whether the form-of-life hypothesis can be applied to other psy chiatric kinds should be explored elsewhere, it should not be taken to automatically apply to any. After all, the traits and harms associated with different disorders are unique in each case. For instance, although both autism and psychopathy are often lumped together as empathy disorders, a near consensus has emerged in recent years that the empathis ing problems associated with each are importantly different (see, e.g. Baron-Cohen 2011; Bollard 2013). So no claim about any single case should be taken to automatically apply to others, even if they initially seem to have some important similarities.
